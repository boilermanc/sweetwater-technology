# Inquiry retention procedure

Owner: Clint Crowe. Approved October 18, 2026: 12 months after the last meaningful contact for inquiries that do not become projects, reviewed monthly. This is a manual procedure, not a scheduled deletion job. Publishing the policy does not delete records.

## Monthly review

1. Review closed/lost/inactive inquiries and their related records. Use the last substantive conversation, reply or call as the last meaningful contact. Automated reminders, campaigns, delivery retries and internal edits do not restart the clock. If there is no later contact, use the original inquiry date. Do not infer inactivity solely from a database updated timestamp.
2. Exclude active projects and necessary legal, dispute or security holds. Record the reason, scope and next review date for any exception. Keep only the records necessary for that exception. Project and accounting records follow their separate contractual/legal requirements; do not apply the inquiry rule indiscriminately.
3. For an eligible inquiry, inventory the database inquiry and conversation, portal notes, email notifications and correspondence, completed/cancelled calendar entries, Slack alerts, and automation execution history or exported copies if any. Also check provider-held message copies under our control. Do not expose unrelated calendar details or delete active appointments, credentials, booking claim locks, transaction/audit records, or shared tables as a shortcut.
4. Prepare exact record identifiers and a deletion/anonymization plan. Database work must use the repository's Switchboard route and migration authority. Obtain the explicit write/deletion approvals required by the working agreements. Verify dependencies and recovery before changes. If targeted removal is unsafe, retain only the necessary restricted records and document why while resolving it.
5. Remove or irreversibly anonymize eligible operational records and matching copies. Merely moving mail to Trash is not final disposal; follow provider recovery periods and separately confirm any irreversible purge. Process a verified privacy request promptly under applicable deadlines rather than waiting for the next monthly review.
6. Record the review date, reviewed date range, systems checked, counts, exceptions and next review date. Avoid retaining the deleted message body or contact details in the review log. Retain a minimal deletion reference only where needed to prevent restoration or demonstrate handling. If a backup is restored, reapply approved deletions before returning the data to routine use. Provider backup rotation and independently required security records are not represented as immediate deletion.

The first review should occur in November 2026. The site does not currently send a review reminder or run automatic deletion. Clint is responsible for carrying out the monthly review. No records were deleted when these pages were published.

## Policy evidence and limits

The pages describe the inspected contact/booking code and deployment records: IONOS, Cloudflare Turnstile, n8n, Supabase, Resend, Google Calendar/Meet/Gmail, Slack alerts and the checked-in Gemini Sage configuration. No blanket no-training claim is made because the Gemini billing/data-use configuration was not verified. No automatic 12-month cleanup was found or added. Provider-specific retention remains subject to provider controls and terms.

Background: FTC data minimization and written retention guidance: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/protecting-personal-information-guide-business-0 . Google distinguishes paid/unpaid data use: https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms . Turnstile notice: https://www.cloudflare.com/turnstile-privacy-policy/ . The 12-month period is the owner's approved business policy, not a universal statutory deadline.

Release checks: published Terms/Privacy have an effective date, working footer and cross-links, crawler-readable HTML, canonical metadata and sitemap entries. Previous draft text remains in Git history. Rollback may restore the previous website release, but must not be used to retroactively weaken privacy promises for previously collected information.
